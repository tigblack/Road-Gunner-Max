Visual direction
A compact open desert buggy with rust-brown and faded orange scrap armor,
an exposed steel roll cage, oversized knobby tires, visible suspension arms,
a rear-mounted engine and exhausts, a strapped fuel tank, and a toothed ram.
A seated red-bandanna raider driver, cream identification marks, rivets and
paint chips keep the vehicle consistent with Scrap Rush's cartoon wasteland.
Real procedural 3D geometry and existing game materials; no concept image
is used as a model or background.

Behavior
Three preallocated buggies spawn at editable scene markers alongside the six
raider groups. The normal initial population is 24 raiders + 3 buggies = 27.
Both enemy types share the existing 30-hostile limit. Markers remain editable
under SpawnDirector > Buggy spawn markers.
620 kg Rigidbody, four WheelColliders, wheel pose updates and suspension.
NavMesh paths guide physical steering, with obstacle probes and a short reverse
maneuver if movement stalls. Normal movement never teleports the buggy.
Aggro radius 26 m; maximum desired speed 11 m/s. The buggy slows and stops
at a firing gap about 7.5 m, retreats if the player comes within 5.5 m, and
chooses visible firing positions around scenery. It has no collision attack
or ram damage. Physical vehicle collisions still prevent interpenetration.
The rotating rusty roof machine gun shares the player's 10 m attack radius.
Default weapon: 2 rounds/s, 5 damage per hit, 0.8 s warmup, orange tracers.
Destruction produces a pooled cartoon explosion with fire, dust, soot, sparks,
metal debris, a ground ring and sound; armor is blackened until the next spawn.
See RANGED_ENEMIES_AND_GEAR.txt and Ranged-Loot-2026-10-07 for the latest update.
The player's own front ram and roof turret can damage the buggy. World geometry
still blocks turret shots. Target selection supports raiders and buggies.
105 health, three scrap for one destruction, a brief smoking wreck, then pooled
reuse. Respawn waits 16 seconds, checks navigation/clearance, and requires the
marker to be outside the camera and at least 20 m from the player.
Pause, defeat and restart handle both enemy types. Reward accounting supports
multiple scrap units per enemy without changing pickup capacity.

Authoring
EnemyBuggyBuilder.Install adds the enemy to both named gameplay scenes and
refuses to discard dirty scenes. Repeating it does not duplicate buggy pools.
Build Prototype includes the enemy automatically. The existing Level01 scene
retains its reference-pending status; this addition does not claim to reproduce
the inaccessible video or apply its requested footprint scale.
RG_Buggy is a dedicated collision layer. Buggies physically collide with the
player and world, while allied pedestrian raiders do not push the buggy.
Player handling, input controller, camera and UI geometry remain intact.

Verification
Original addition baseline (before the ranged-only update): 43 checks passed: 11 enemy-buggy checks, 16 joystick/control checks,
and 16 vehicle/combat/session checks (suite completion markers excluded).
Buggy checks cover all three initial spawns and the shared cap, four grounded
wheels, physical pursuit, actual collision ram damage, navigation around an
existing rock, blocked and unblocked turret shots, one death reward, pooled
wreck cleanup, the player's ram, distant respawn/near-player guard, pause and
full restart. The ram test dealt exactly one 18-point hit; the rock-route test
reduced target distance from 24.2 to 3.9 m without overturning the buggy.
Control checks include all eight joystick headings, a forward U-turn, S reverse,
pointer priority and touch cancellation. Reset diagnostics now synchronize
physics after despawning all enemy vehicles and restoring the player's pose.
No missing/unsupported materials, C# errors, shader errors or new game/scene
Console errors. The cloned editor emitted its previously observed Unity
SearchDatabase startup indexing exception. No physical mobile device or mobile
frame-rate claim is made.
